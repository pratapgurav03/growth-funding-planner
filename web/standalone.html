Where the return comes from

DuPont identity from chapter 3: return on equity is profit margin times asset turnover times the equity multiplier.

Statements

Blue numbers are typed in. The right-most column is the base year the forecast starts from. Units do not matter as long as every number uses the same ones.

Statements update the results as you type. Forecast and Valuation wait for Apply changes.

Import from a spreadsheet paste, CSV or 10-K text

Paste rows copied from Excel, Google Sheets, a 10-K table or a PDF, or upload a CSV. Lines are matched to the rows above by name, then you review before anything is replaced. Matching is best effort: check every number.

Sales growth and assumptions

The book takes the growth rate as given. A set of statements only shows the past, so the planner offers a trend from your history, or you can type your own rate.

    Ending balances, as in chapter 3. The pro forma column uses the projected statements after financing.

    Limits. A trend from past sales is not a prediction. The model is an accounting statement generator: it keeps margins and turnover constant and leaves out cash flow timing, risk and value (Ross, section 4.5). Taxes are not credited on losses. Example Co. numbers are invented.

    Internal growth rate = ROA x b / (1 - ROA x b). Sustainable growth rate = ROE x b / (1 - ROE x b), with ending equity. Checked against the book's Rosengarten, Hoffman and Skandia examples by an automated test suite.
